# States & Capitals Challenge — Design Exploration

## Three Possible Directions

### 1. Atlas Adventure Club
**Very Brief Intro:** A warm, tactile field-journal experience that makes each state feel like a place a young explorer can discover. It feels curious, capable, and a little bit collectible.

**Probability:** 0.07

### 2. Roadtrip Sticker Book
**Very Brief Intro:** A bright, playful scrapbook with highway signs, postcard stamps, and colorful state badges. The mood is energetic, friendly, and competition-ready.

**Probability:** 0.04

### 3. Museum Map Lab
**Very Brief Intro:** A calm, contemporary learning studio where maps, labels, and answer cards are treated like hands-on exhibits. It feels focused and smart without being serious.

**Probability:** 0.09

## Chosen Direction — Atlas Adventure Club

### Design Movement
**Modern Americana field journal**, borrowing from National Park trail guides, screen-printed transit graphics, and kid-friendly natural history exhibits.

### Core Principles
1. **Learn by exploring:** The state map stays visually central, with every action pointing back to geography.
2. **Earn a trail of progress:** Stars, stamps, and a clear stage path reward practice without adding pressure.
3. **Confident clarity:** Big labels, high contrast, and one unmistakable action at a time make the experience friendly for young learners.
4. **Tactile discovery:** Paper-like surfaces, imperfect edge details, and illustrated markers make the interface feel collected rather than generic.

### Color Philosophy
The primary canvas is a **sun-warmed parchment** that feels like a well-loved atlas. Deep navy anchors names and navigation like map ink, while pine green signals exploration and correct answers. A bold **canyon orange** creates energetic calls to action and celebratory milestones. Clear sky blue is used as a supportive learning cue, never as a dominant wash.

### Layout Paradigm
The page is a **two-column expedition desk** rather than a centered dashboard: a persistent illustrated map and progress rail sit beside a changing learning desk. On smaller screens, the map becomes a compact location card above the active task. The stage route is a visible trail, not a standard tab bar.

### Signature Elements
1. A **dashed expedition route** links the three stages and the completion marker.
2. Each score is represented by a **trail badge** and star burst, instead of a plain number alone.
3. **Field-note cards** use a distinctive clipped-corner silhouette, tiny coordinate labels, and topographic line texture.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should feel like placing a pin on a map or checking off a trail guide. Cards lift slightly and buttons have a brief, firm press response. Correct answers produce a short badge pop; incorrect answers offer a helpful clue and a gentle retry path rather than a harsh failure moment.

### Animation
The dashed route draws in only when a stage is unlocked. Cards enter with a 40–70 ms stagger and a short upward movement. Answer feedback uses 160–220 ms opacity and transform transitions, never continuous decorative movement. Celebration uses a brief star scatter on milestone completion, with all non-essential movement disabled for reduced-motion preferences.

### Typography System
**Fraunces** is the expressive map-title voice: heavy, slightly soft, and used only for headlines and state names. **Nunito Sans** is the warm, highly legible body voice used for questions, buttons, and scores. Titles use a compact 700–800 weight; instructional copy is 600; interface labels use spaced 800 uppercase sparingly to echo map legends.

### Brand Essence
**A colorful geography trail that helps young explorers learn America’s states, capitals, and locations—then prove what they know.**

Personality: **Curious, encouraging, adventurous.**

### Brand Voice
Headlines are active and geographic; CTAs sound like invitations to explore. Microcopy celebrates effort and tells the learner exactly what happens next.

> “Pin the capital. Claim the trail.”

> “You found the Pacific Northwest—ready to name its capital?”

### Wordmark & Logo
The wordmark uses a stout, slightly arched **TrailTrek** lockup, paired with a bold graphic mark: a navy compass point folded around a canyon-orange map pin and a small sky-blue star. The mark works alone as the favicon and is kept large enough to read clearly.

### Signature Brand Color
**Trail Orange — `#F26B38`**: a warm, high-energy orange reserved for progress, primary actions, and earned achievement.
